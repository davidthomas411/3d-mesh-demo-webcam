Webcam to skeleton

A live webcam feed becomes a 3-D body mesh, with a skeleton, a simulated X-ray and a CT view that move with the person. This page explains each stage and what the one-time SKEL set-up does.

The whole pipeline

runs live, every framebuilt once, saved to disk

Live path, step by step

1

Webcam frame

The browser grabs a frame and sends it as a JPEG over a WebSocket to the local server. The camera only works on localhost or 127.0.0.1.

2

Find the person

A YOLO11 pose detector draws a box around the person and keeps following the same one. MoGe2 estimates camera field of view and depth, so the mesh lands at the right size and distance. The log line FOCAL_CALIBRATED is that calibration.

3

Fit a body mesh

Fast SAM 3D Body predicts the person's pose and shape and outputs a body mesh in the MHR model's format: 18,439 vertices, 36,874 faces, plus a joint skeleton. The DINOv3 backbone runs at 384 px. Inference was about 165 ms on your RTX A2000.

4

Stream to the page

The server sends the vertices as raw float32 bytes. The page draws the mesh over the video and in its own rotatable 3-D view.

5

Pose the anatomy

Bones are bound to MHR joints. When the joints move, the bones move with them (linear blend skinning). The page then draws the bones as a skeleton, as a simulated radiograph (DRR), and as a CT slice.

Where SKEL fits in

SKEL is a research skeleton model (Keller et al. 2023) learned from many bodies. The page needs it because the MHR mesh has no bones inside. Three files are produced once and loaded by the page.

A

Neutral rest body make_mhr_rest.py

Reads model.ckpt and mhr_model.pt and writes mhr_rest.npz: the MHR body standing in a neutral pose (vertices, faces, rest keypoints). It is the reference everything else is fitted to.

B

Fit SKEL to that body fit_skel_to_mhr.py

Uses mhr2smpl_mapping.npz to turn the MHR surface into the SMPL topology SKEL expects, then adjusts SKEL's shape until its skin matches. Your run gave mean 13.2 mm, median 10.7 mm. Output: skel_fit_mhr_rest.npz, about 247k skeleton vertices.

C

Build the atlas build_skel_atlas.py

Simplifies each bone mesh (with fast_simplification), binds it to the MHR joints, and writes skel.json (structure) and skel.bin (geometry). It runs for the default atlas and for each reference subject.

Generic, not personal. The skeleton is fitted to the average MHR body, not to a CT and not to the person on camera. The person's live pose drives it through the MHR joints, but bone sizes come from the average body. The reference CT is registered onto the SKEL spine, so the skeleton is the common frame.

Glossary

MHRThe body model Fast SAM 3D Body outputs: mesh plus joints.
SKELA learned skeleton model with realistic bones and joints, fitted inside a skin surface.
AtlasPre-built anatomy (bone meshes and their joint binding) that is posed at run time.
DRRDigitally reconstructed radiograph: a simulated X-ray made from bones or CT voxels.
SkinningMoving a mesh by blending the motion of nearby joints.
TensorRT engineA GPU-specific compiled copy of the backbone. Build one per PC.
